Cosmology and modified gravitational wave propagation from binary black hole population models

arXiv:2112.05728 · open the paper whenever the snippets are not enough
Abstract

A joint hierarchical Bayesian analysis of the binary black hole (BBH) mass function, merger rate evolution, and cosmological parameters can be used to extract information on both the cosmological and population parameters. We extend this technique to include the effect of modified gravitational wave (GW) propagation. We discuss the constraints on the parameter Ξ<SUB>0</SUB> that describe this phenomenon [with Ξ<SUB>0</SUB>=1 in general relativity (GR)] using the data from the third gravitational wave transient catalog. We find the constraints Ξ<SUB>0</SUB>=1. 2<SUB>-0.7</SUB><SUP>+0.7</SUP> with a flat prior on Ξ<SUB>0</SUB>, and Ξ<SUB>0</SUB>=1. 0<SUB>-0.8</SUB><SUP>+0.4</SUP> with a prior uniform in log Ξ<SUB>0</SUB> (68% C.L., maximum posterior and highest-density interval), which only rely on the presence of a feature in the BBH mass distribution around ∼30 - 45 M<SUB>☉</SUB> and are robust to whether or not the event GW190521 is considered an outlier of the population. We then study in more detail the effects of modified GW propagation on population and cosmological analyses for LIGO/Virgo at design sensitivity. We find that, for a given data-taking period, the relative error ∆ Ξ<SUB>0</SUB>/Ξ<SUB>0</SUB> has a significant dependence on the fiducial value of Ξ<SUB>0</SUB>, since the latter has a strong influence on the detection rate. For five years of data, the accuracy ranges from ∼10 % on Ξ<SUB>0</SUB> when Ξ<SUB>0</SUB>=1 to ∆ Ξ<SUB>0</SUB>/Ξ<SUB>0</SUB>∼20 % for Ξ<SUB>0</SUB>=1.8 , which represents a large deviation from GR, still consistent with current limits and predicted by viable cosmological models. For the Hubble parameter, we forecast an accuracy of ∆ H<SUB>0</SUB>/H<SUB>0</SUB>∼20 % and an accuracy on H (z ) of ∼7 % at a pivot redshift z<SUB>*</SUB>∼0.8 ; this updates the results found in previous studies, by making use of a population model compatible with current observations, and is about a factor 2 worse, in twice the observing time. We finally show that, if nature is described by a modified gravity theory with a large deviation from the GR value Ξ<SUB>0</SUB>=1 , such as Ξ<SUB>0</SUB>=1.8 , analyzing the data assuming GR produces a significant bias in the inferred values of the mass scales, Hubble constant, and particularly the BBH merger rate.
